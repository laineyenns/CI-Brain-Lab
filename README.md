# Neurophysiological Correlates of Top-Down Alpha Oscillatory Modulation and Neural Entrainment as Mechanisms of Attention in Children Cochlear Implant Patients

**Author:** Lainey Enns
**Lab:** Cochlear Implant (CI) Brain Lab, Otolaryngology – Head and Neck Surgery, Sunnybrook Health Sciences Centre
**Supervisor:** Dr. Andrew Dimitrijevic
**Period:** May–December 2023

## Abstract

Recruitment and modulation of neural networks facilitates and suppresses particular sensorineural processing mechanisms, with extensive recent audiological research focusing on selective attention. A mechanism of unconscious automaticity, or minimal effort voluntarily, in normal hearing individuals, is disrupted and cognitively taxing in those with hearing impairments. Cochlear implants succeed in providing absolute hearing improvement providing greater functionality and quality of life, however research is inconclusive as to how moderate to severe sensorineural hearing loss specifically alters these mechanisms and how cochlear implants lack the ability to compensate. We consider and examine how top-down modulation and phase sequential modulation may influence enhancement and suppression of attention and correlate to self perceived speech comprehension and cognitive demand in CI patients. Accordingly, we will record high density electroencephalograms (EEG) while CI users listen/watch episodes of the TV show, Malcolm in the Middle, in a cocktail party effect paradigm. Multi-babble talk will project from masking speakers in three randomized conditions of left, right or collocated, to parallel an environment of multiple sound sources. A quiet condition will also be executed as a baseline and to contrast the high perceptual load conditions. Subsequent self perceived cognitive demand and speech/content comprehension tests of trials will be performed. Follow-up to completion of all trials a visuospatial memory test will be executed to measure individual working memory capacity. Processing of continuous EEG recordings will be used to quantify neural entrainment (i.e., TRFs, temporal response functions) to the attend, episode, and ignore, babble talk, speech envelopes and to measure magnitude and localization of alpha oscillations, 8–12 Hz, associated with attentional effort.
